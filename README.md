# Quarter Section

**A family, a homestead, a century.** 1890 to 2060, one household on 160 acres, and a clock
that stops to ask.

A standalone historical life-sim by **Raven Iron**. This repository holds **releases only** —
the source lives in a separate private repo.

---

## Play it

- **In the browser:** <https://ravenirongames.com/play/quarter-section/> — Chrome, Edge or
  Firefox on a desktop. About 16 MB loads once; nothing is installed.
- **On Windows:** grab the zip from [Releases](../../releases/latest), unzip anywhere and run
  `QuarterSection.exe`.

The build is not signed, so Windows may show *"Windows protected your PC"*: click **More info**,
then **Run anyway**.

Saves are per family — in your browser's storage for this site, or under your user profile's
AppData on Windows — written at every season's start and after every answered card. A save from
0.8 is brought forward.

## What it is

A quarter section is the 160 acres a family got under the Homestead Act, if they could hold it.
You get one in 1890: a log house, a well, an outhouse, three plots, and a town a day's walk away.
Every decade after gives a little more.

**The 1890s are meant to hurt.** Water is carried by the bucket, the housework takes half of every
day, and a birth or a fever with the man away in town can undo a year. Then the wire reaches the
street, the doctor comes to town, the car cuts the road to three hours, and the century's own
troubles arrive on schedule: the flu of 1918, the crash, the wars, the long summer.

**The clock stops to ask.** A fever, the first snow with the pile short, the harvest week with a
thin larder, the county's due the tin could not cover. The game stops and offers two to four
answers, each priced in what the house actually holds. Every birth can be named; every death is
noted, with its cause and its cost.

**The numbers are real.** Every wage, price and tax is a published series in constant 1890
dollars: a farm hand's day, a gallon of kerosene, 160 acres of county tax, a doctor's call, the
Model T. The people are not — every name comes from a list.

## This is an alpha

Every run is reproducible from its number. When something is wrong, or just odd, open an issue
with the **version, the land's number and the year** from the title and naming screens, and what
happened.

> *"0.9.0, land 17, 1903, the county's due card said I owed twelve dollars I had"*

That is enough to replay the exact run.

Also welcome: where it was boring, where a card asked something you had no way to judge, and what
you wished the household could do. The 1890s are meant to hurt — say if they hurt for a reason
that felt unfair.
